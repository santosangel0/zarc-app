# Thermal Stress in Dairy Cattle

Thermal stress (estresse calórico) occurs when environmental heat load exceeds the animal's capacity to dissipate body heat, triggering physiological responses that reduce milk production, reproductive performance, and overall welfare.

## Thermoneutral Zones by Breed

Heat stress response is **breed-dependent** (Bernabucci et al. 2010; McManus et al. 2011):

| Breed Group | Thermoneutral Zone | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Zebu** (Bos indicus) | 10–32°C | Better heat tolerance, but can be eroded by selection for high milk yield (Santana Jr. et al. 2015) |
| **Holstein** (Bos taurus) | 4–26°C | More sensitive to heat; higher production potential under comfort |
| **Guzerá** (dual-purpose Zebu) | — | Exhibits high dairy performance even under heat stress (Santana et al. 2020) |

**Key finding**: continuous selection for high milk production in Zebu breeds can deteriorate their natural heat tolerance — a genetic antagonism between production level and thermal stress response ([[andrade-2023-thi-maps-southeast-brazil|Andrade et al. 2023]]; Santana Jr. et al. 2015).

## Mechanisms

- **Above upper critical temperature**: the animal increases thermolysis (heat dissipation via respiration and cutaneous evaporation).
- **High humidity** reduces evaporative cooling capacity, compounding the effect of high temperature.
- **Solar radiation** adds an external heat increment to metabolic heat load (Ferreira et al. 2017).

## Impact on Dairy Production

- Milk yield declines when [[thi-temperature-humidity-index|THI]] ≥ 72.
- In southeastern Brazil, the correlation between summer THI and mesoregion-level milk productivity is r = 0.66 ([[andrade-2023-thi-maps-southeast-brazil|Andrade et al. 2023]]).
- Reproductive performance also declines under heat stress (Rensis & Scaramuzzi 2003).

## Management Implications

- **Territorial management** (gestão territorial): matching breed genetics to the climate profile of each region — zoning dairy breeds by thermal tolerance.
- Shade, ventilation, cooling systems for intensive operations.
- For extensive pasture systems, breed selection and seasonal management are the primary levers.
