# Precipitation and Dairy Production

The relationship between precipitation and dairy production operates primarily through **forage availability** — rainfall drives pasture growth, which in turn determines feed supply and quality for dairy herds, particularly in extensive and semi-intensive systems.

## Evidence

### Direct Production Correlation

[[dias-souza-2026-dairy-climate-minas-gerais|Dias-Souza et al. (2026)]] found that in high-productivity dairy cities of Minas Gerais:

- **Precipitation explains ~83% of milk production variation** (quadratic polynomial regression, R² = 0.8993, p = 0.0076)
- Each 1 mm increase in precipitation → ~73 liters increase in milk production
- Temperature alone had **no significant effect** (R² = 0.1995, p = 0.71)
- Patos de Minas (highest precipitation at 1,940.6 mm/year) had the highest production index

This suggests that in the moderate temperature range of these cities (21–23°C), water availability is the binding constraint — not heat.

### Vegetation Response to Drought

[[silva-2025-drought-indices-paracatu|Silva et al. (2025)]] demonstrated that the 6-month [[spi-spei-drought-indices|SPI]] correlates strongly with [[ndvi-vegetation-indices|NDVI]] (r = 0.89) in the Cerrado, with a 2-month lag. This confirms the causal chain: precipitation deficit → vegetation decline → reduced forage → production impact.

[[leivas-2014-svi-drought-soybean-south-brazil|Leivas et al. (2014)]] showed the same pattern for soybean in Southern Brazil: drought years (2004/05, 2008/09, 2011/12) produced large areas of below-normal vegetation vigor.

## Interaction with Thermal Stress

Precipitation and [[thermal-stress-dairy|thermal stress]] are not independent:

- **Hot + dry** conditions compound stress: heat reduces intake while drought reduces forage quality
- High temperatures increase evapotranspiration, worsening water deficit even at the same precipitation level — this is why the [[spi-spei-drought-indices|SPEI]] (which accounts for ET) exists
- However, in practice, the simpler SPI performs better for vegetation correlation in the Cerrado ([[silva-2025-drought-indices-paracatu|Silva et al. 2025]])

## Implications for ZARC

A comprehensive dairy climate risk zoning should include **both** thermal stress ([[thi-temperature-humidity-index|THI]]) **and** water deficit (SPI or precipitation metrics) as risk dimensions. The evidence suggests that in some regions, water availability may be more limiting than heat.
